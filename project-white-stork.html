White Stork Migration Analysis

Behavioral state classification on 54,613 GPS locations from 42 white storks using Hidden Markov Models, with conditional logit habitat selection analysis.

Python R HMM Conditional Logit GPS Data Statistical Modeling
GitHub → ← Back to Projects

Overview

A joint research project with Universität Bielefeld and the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, studying the migratory behavior of white storks through statistical modeling of GPS tracking data.

Problem

Raw GPS coordinates don't directly tell you what an animal is doing — resting, foraging, or in transit. Turning a stream of latitude/longitude fixes into meaningful behavioral states requires a statistical model of the underlying (unobserved) behavior driving the observed movement.

Approach

Hidden Markov Models were fit to 54,613 GPS locations collected from 42 tagged white storks, treating behavioral state as a latent variable inferred from movement patterns such as speed and turning angle between consecutive fixes. Once behavioral states were classified, conditional logit models were used to analyze habitat selection — which environments storks preferred conditional on which behavioral state they were in.

Results

Behavioral state sequences were classified across the full GPS tracks of all 42 individuals, and habitat preferences were quantified separately for each inferred behavioral state, rather than as one undifferentiated average.

Challenges

GPS fix intervals were irregular across individuals and time periods, which complicates the standard HMM assumption of evenly spaced observations. Distinguishing 'resting' from 'foraging' states in particular required engineering auxiliary movement-speed features, since both states can look similar in raw position data alone.
